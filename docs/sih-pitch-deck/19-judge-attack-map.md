## Slide 2 — IDEA TITLE

| Challenge | Pre-empted? | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| *"Isn't this just tshark with extra steps?"* | ✅ on slide | tshark dissects fields; it produces no security verdict, no cross-session reasoning, no evidence states. tshark is our dependency, not our competitor. |
| *"Cross-session correlation isn't new — Zeek does this."* | ⚠️ **partially** | Correct, and our wording concedes it: the claim is scoped to *"five competing implementations audited"*, not to the NSM field. **Rehearse this answer** — it is the most likely sharp challenge. |

| Challenge | Pre-empted? | Answer |

## Slide 4 — FEASIBILITY AND VIABILITY

| Challenge | Pre-empted? | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| *"1219 tests — what's the coverage?"* | ❌ | **Q&A:** coverage percentage was never measured; we don't claim one. The tests include adversarial cases asserting forbidden inferences cannot occur. |
| *"10 captures is a small corpus."* | ⚠️ partially | **Q&A:** agreed — two vendors, loopback-generated. Stated as a limitation. The golden corpus adds 25 scenario captures. |
| *"You can't see certificates at all in your real data?"* | ✅ on slide | Correct, and it's on the slide as a measured result: 0 of 10, because TLS 1.3 encrypts it. Certificate capability is validated on generated TLS 1.2 fixtures, labelled as such. |
| *"So certificate analysis is untested on real traffic?"* | ✅ on slide | Yes — stated explicitly rather than hidden. |
| *"Why is D-11 partial? Just bundle a CA store."* | ⚠️ partially | **Q&A:** we evaluated that and rejected it — enterprise mail routinely uses private CAs, so a public root store would flag legitimate deployments as untrusted. That's a systematic false positive on exactly our target population. |
| *"Isn't admitting limitations a weakness?"* | ✅ by design | The template asks for risks. Naming real, measured ones instead of invented soft ones is the strongest credibility signal available. |

## The three challenges most likely to be fatal if unrehearsed

1. **"Zeek already does cross-session correlation."** → Concede immediately and precisely: yes, in
   general NSM; our scoped claim is about the mail-forensics implementations audited. Never defend
   an absolute novelty claim, because we never made one.
2. **"Your AI doesn't detect anything."** → Correct, and we say so. Pivot to: the deterministic
   engine is the detector; the AI is bounded prioritisation; and `--no-ai` equivalence is *proven*,
   which almost no competing implementation tested at all.
3. **"You can't see certificates in real traffic."** → Correct, measured, on the slide. TLS 1.3
   constrains every passive observer equally. We extract fully where TLS ≤1.2 permits, and we say
   `NOT_OBSERVABLE` with the reason rather than guessing.

**Pattern:** every fatal challenge is answered by *conceding the accurate part immediately* and
redirecting to what was actually built. The deck is designed so that none of these is a surprise.
